# Preliminary Adjusted Survey-Weighted Logistic Model

## Model

Outcome: any anaemia versus no anaemia.

Analytic sample: **7,550 women** after excluding seven women with unavailable/flagged BMI information.

The model included:

- age group
- education
- household wealth quintile
- urban/rural residence
- current pregnancy
- parity
- BMI category
- current employment
- marital status
- region

Coefficients were estimated using sampling weights. Standard errors were calculated with a stratified primary-sampling-unit sandwich estimator using the DHS strata and clusters.

These are preliminary association estimates and should not be interpreted causally.

## Selected adjusted odds ratios

| Factor | Comparison | Adjusted OR | 95% CI |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Pregnancy | Currently pregnant vs no/unsure | **1.78** | **1.43–2.21** |
| BMI | Obesity vs normal weight | **0.60** | **0.49–0.72** |
| BMI | Overweight vs normal weight | **0.73** | **0.62–0.87** |
| BMI | Underweight vs normal weight | 1.19 | 0.96–1.47 |
| Residence | Rural vs urban | 1.04 | 0.88–1.22 |
| Region | Bono vs Greater Accra | **0.62** | **0.42–0.91** |
| Region | Oti vs Greater Accra | **1.48** | **1.11–1.99** |

## Wealth quintile after multivariable adjustment

Reference: poorest quintile.

| Wealth quintile | Adjusted OR | 95% CI |
|---|---:|---:|
| Poorer | 0.91 | 0.76–1.10 |
| Middle | 0.98 | 0.77–1.23 |
| Richer | 0.98 | 0.76–1.25 |
| Richest | 1.03 | 0.77–1.36 |

The strong unadjusted socioeconomic gradient is therefore attenuated after simultaneous adjustment for demographic, reproductive, nutritional, and geographic characteristics.

This does **not** imply absence of socioeconomic inequality. The concentration index remains negative and statistically distinguishable from zero. Rather, it suggests that observed wealth-related inequality may operate partly through differences in characteristics such as nutritional status, pregnancy composition, and geography.

## Education after multivariable adjustment

Reference: no education.

| Education | Adjusted OR | 95% CI |

| Primary | 1.03 | 0.83–1.27 |

## Interpretation

The adjusted model reinforces the rationale for inequality decomposition. A crude socioeconomic gradient can coexist with weak conditional wealth coefficients when the variables through which inequality is distributed are included in the model.

The next planned analysis is decomposition of the wealth-related concentration index, followed by sensitivity analyses of the outcome definition and pregnancy subgroup.
