# Sensitivity and Geographic Checks

## BMI specification sensitivity

The preliminary inequality decomposition identified BMI / nutritional status as the largest measured contributor to wealth-related anaemia inequality.

To assess whether that finding depended on modelling BMI as a continuous nonlinear term, the decomposition was repeated using clinically interpretable BMI categories with **normal weight as the reference**:

- underweight
- normal weight
- overweight
- obesity

When BMI was entered categorically, the combined BMI-domain contribution remained large:

- Absolute contribution to the Erreygers index: **-0.0430**
- Share of total observed inequality: **73.1%**

This is close to the **77.8%** contribution obtained with the nonlinear continuous BMI specification.

### Interpretation

The strong BMI contribution is therefore not simply an artifact of one functional form. It reflects two simultaneous patterns in the data:

1. anaemia prevalence declines substantially as BMI rises; and
2. higher BMI categories are disproportionately concentrated among wealthier women.

This should still be interpreted as a statistical decomposition rather than evidence that increasing BMI would causally reduce anaemia.

## Pregnancy sensitivity

Among women not currently pregnant, weighted anaemia prevalence was approximately **40.4%** and wealth-related inequality remained pro-poor.

The pregnancy subgroup is comparatively small, so pregnancy-stratified inequality estimates will be treated as sensitivity analyses rather than primary results.

## Implications for the main paper

These checks strengthen three parts of the emerging story:

1. **Socioeconomic inequality:** anaemia remains concentrated among poorer women.
2. **Nutritional patterning:** the large BMI contribution is robust to alternative BMI parameterisation.
3. **Geographic inequality:** there is substantial regional heterogeneity that persists beyond a simple urban-rural contrast.

The next step is to quantify residual community-level heterogeneity and then finalize the decomposition uncertainty analysis.
