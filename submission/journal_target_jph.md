# Target Journal: Journal of Public Health (Oxford University Press)

## Recommendation

**Primary target:** *Journal of Public Health* (Oxford University Press)

### Why it fits

- Publishes original research across the full spectrum of public health.
- Accepts studies from any region of the world.
- Prioritizes scientific originality, public-health impact, and translation of evidence into action.
- The study's focus on socioeconomic inequality, geographic heterogeneity, and implications for targeted anaemia control aligns well with the journal's remit.
- Standard subscription publication is available without an open-access publication charge.

## Current submission requirements relevant to this paper

### Article type
**Original Paper**

### Main-text length
**2,000–3,000 words**

### Tables and figures
Maximum of **4 tables/figures combined** in the main paper.

Recommended main-paper allocation:

1. **Table I** — weighted sample characteristics and anaemia prevalence.
2. **Table II** — adjusted survey-weighted logistic regression.
3. **Figure 1** — concentration curve.
4. **Figure 2** — regional anaemia prevalence.

Move to supplementary material:

- decomposition table;
- decomposition figure;
- wealth-gradient figure;
- full multilevel variance-component table;
- sensitivity analyses;
- bootstrap details.

### Abstract
Structured under:

- Background
- Methods
- Results
- Conclusions

Maximum **200 words**.

### Discussion structure
The journal specifically requests these subheadings:

1. **Main finding of this study**
2. **What is already known on this topic**
3. **What this study adds**
4. **Limitations of this study**

### Reporting guideline
The study is observational and should follow **STROBE**.

### References
Numbered consecutively in Arabic numerals using an Index Medicus/Vancouver-style format.

### Tables
Tables should be numbered using **Roman numerals**.

### Figures
Figures must be separate files. Alt text is required for each figure.

## Publication-cost strategy

The journal offers optional open access. If open access is not selected, the article can be published under standard subscription access **without an OA charge**.

## Submission positioning

The strongest editorial pitch is not simply that anaemia is common in Ghana. The novelty is the integration of:

- nationally representative prevalence estimation;
- wealth-related concentration analysis;
- decomposition of inequality;
- geographic heterogeneity;
- community-level random-effects analysis.

The paper should emphasize that conventional determinant models can obscure population-level inequality and that inequality metrics add actionable information for targeting public-health interventions.
