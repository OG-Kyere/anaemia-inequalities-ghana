# Study Protocol

## Working title

**Socioeconomic and Geographic Inequalities in Anaemia Among Women of Reproductive Age in Ghana**

## Background

Anaemia remains an important public health concern among women of reproductive age in Ghana. Its distribution may reflect socioeconomic, nutritional, reproductive, and geographic inequalities. A national analysis that combines inequality measurement with multilevel modelling can help distinguish individual-level disparities from broader contextual variation.

## Primary objective

To quantify socioeconomic inequality in anaemia among women aged 15–49 years in Ghana.

## Secondary objectives

1. To describe the prevalence and severity of anaemia across socioeconomic, demographic, reproductive, nutritional, and geographic groups.
2. To identify factors contributing to wealth-related inequality in anaemia.
3. To estimate individual- and community-level associations with anaemia.
4. To assess whether residual geographic and cluster-level variation persists after adjustment for measured characteristics.
5. To evaluate the robustness of findings across alternative outcome definitions and model specifications.

## Study design

Secondary cross-sectional analysis of nationally representative survey data from the 2022 Ghana Demographic and Health Survey.

## Study population

Women aged 15–49 years who were eligible for haemoglobin testing and have a valid anaemia or haemoglobin measurement.

## Primary outcome

Anaemia status, derived from the DHS anaemia classification variable.

Primary coding:

- 0 = not anaemic
- 1 = mild, moderate, or severe anaemia

Secondary analyses will examine anaemia severity.

## Main exposures

The principal socioeconomic exposure will be household wealth rank/quintile.

Other candidate explanatory variables include:

- age
- education
- employment
- marital status
- urban/rural residence
- region
- pregnancy status
- parity
- body mass index
- contraceptive use
- household characteristics

Final covariate selection will be based on a prespecified conceptual framework and variable availability.

## Survey design

All nationally representative estimates will account for DHS sampling weights, clustering, and stratification.

## Ethics and data governance

The study uses de-identified secondary data obtained under The DHS Program's data-use terms. Restricted DHS microdata will not be deposited in this repository.
